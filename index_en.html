About

Gametogenesis and cell rejuvenation

While genetic information is passed down through generations, the aging of the parent generation is not inherited by its offspring. In other words, during generational turnover, cellular aging markers are dramatically reset. The exact mechanisms behind this biological “rejuvenation” remain a profound mystery.

We are tackling this fundamental question using budding yeast as a model organism. When undergoing meiosis, budding yeast forms next-generation cells known as spores within the mother cell. During this dynamic process, cells undergo not only the precise regulation of gene expression but also a dramatic reorganization of their organelles. We aim to uncover the strategies life employs to achieve this rejuvenation, focusing on the following two perspectives.

Perspective 1

Membrane biogenesis

How is the new membrane that envelops the next generation (the prospore membrane) constructed from scratch? We are investigating this dynamic formation process.

Perspective 2

Cellular sorting

How do cells identify and sort what is essential to pass on to the next generation versus what must be discarded as waste? We are working to elucidate this sophisticated sorting system.
