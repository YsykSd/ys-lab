Research

Research

Using gametogenesis (sporulation) in budding yeast as a model, we study how cells are rejuvenated at each generational turnover, from two angles: membrane formation and organelle sorting.

We study a wide range of phenomena in sporulation of budding yeast.

Theme 1

Membrane biogenesisHow is the prospore membrane born?

How is a new membrane that envelops the next generation built from nothing?

During meiosis, budding yeast builds a new membrane inside the mother cell, the prospore membrane, which engulfs the nuclei and cytoplasm to form four spores. It is a specialized membrane made from scratch by the fusion of vesicles delivered by the secretory pathway.

We combine live-cell imaging and genetics to follow how the prospore membrane extends and takes shape. We have reported that the secretory pathway is remodeled in coordination with prospore membrane formation, and that Rab GTPases and the lipid PI4P are important for membrane formation and extension.

  • Suda Y, Neiman AM (2025) J Fungi 11: 661
  • Suda Y et al. (2024) iScience 27: 110855
  • Nakamura TS, Suda Y et al. (2021) PLoS Genet 17: e1009727
  • Suda Y et al. (2018) FEMS Yeast Res 18: fox095
Theme 2

Cellular sortingWhat is passed on to the next generation?

How do cells decide what to pass on and what to leave behind?

When spores form, not everything in the mother cell is handed over. Some organelles and cellular components are partitioned into the spores, while the rest stays behind in the mother cell.

We focus on how organelles are partitioned and sorted, and how membrane structures that have finished their job disappear, to understand why the aging of the parent is not carried over to the next generation.

  • Suda Y et al. (2007) Eukaryot Cell 6: 2009–2017
  • Nakanishi H, Suda Y, Neiman AM (2007) J Cell Sci 120: 908–916
Theme 3

Image processing and quantitative analysisMeasuring membranes that overlap

How can we accurately measure membranes that overlap in complex ways?

Four prospore membranes change shape while overlapping inside a single cell, which makes measuring each one from microscope images difficult. We developed DeMemSeg, a deep-learning (Mask R-CNN) method that separates and quantifies overlapping prospore membranes, and published a detailed protocol.

  • Taguchi S et al. (2025) Cell Struct Funct 50: 183–195
  • Taguchi S et al. (2025) Bio-protocol 15: e5520
Theme 4

Live imaging of membrane traffic and collaborations

Suda has analyzed the dynamics of the yeast Golgi apparatus and ER–Golgi membrane traffic using super-resolution confocal live imaging microscopy. He continues imaging and image analysis in collaboration with RIKEN.

We also work with colleagues inside and outside the university on gene-expression control by RNA-binding proteins and the biology of wild yeasts.

  • Tojima T, Suda Y et al. (2024) eLife 13
  • Kurokawa K et al. (2019) J Cell Biol 218: 1602–1618
  • Suda Y et al. (2013) PNAS 110: 18976–18981
